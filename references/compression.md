# Cognitive Compression

In every route, preserve both mechanisms and their supporting knowledge.
Keep an internal retention ledger answering: why this compression, what was
lost, and when to recompress. Include concrete mappings to the user's scenes.
Move precise facts to accessible resources rather than abstracting them away.

Compression reduces cognitive complexity while preserving target-relevant generative behavior. Seek minimal complexity subject to adequate Generative Fidelity and Boundary Fidelity.

## Operations
MERGE, SPLIT, DELETE, GENERALIZE, SPECIALIZE.

### DELETE
Delete only when counterfactual removal causes no material target-relevant behavioral, boundary, validation, or reconstruction difference. Frequency is not the criterion.

### MERGE
Merge only when distinctions are behaviorally unnecessary across relevant states. If any relevant state yields different behavior, preserve the distinction. Semantic similarity is insufficient.

### SPLIT
Split when one mechanism produces incompatible behavior under different conditions; search for the hidden discriminating variable.

### GENERALIZE
Replace surface rules with a shared latent causal rule only when it reduces complexity while preserving behavior and boundaries and improving novel-state generation.

### SPECIALIZE
When a mechanism is too broad, add a condition, state variable, boundary, or mode rather than patching examples.

## Compression Error
A simplification that removes a required future discrimination. Typical symptoms: incorrect merge, lost boundary, ambiguous priority, collapsed states, or discarded exception.

## Synergy
Evaluate conditional value, not only isolated value. Information may be weak alone but discriminative jointly.

## Stop rule
Stop when expected complexity reduction is smaller than expected loss of generation, discrimination, boundary accuracy, or auditability. The smallest model is not the one with the fewest words.
